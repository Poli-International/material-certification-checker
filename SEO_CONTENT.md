## What is Biocompatibility Material Checker?

The Biocompatibility Material Checker is a free, client-side reference and validation tool for professional body piercers, studio owners, and jewelry buyers. It answers a specific question that comes up constantly in studio purchasing: **what does a material claim or certificate actually prove, and what is still missing?**

The tool is built around a certification database covering ASTM, ISO, and European standards, including ASTM F136 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI titanium), ASTM F138 (316LVM surgical steel), ASTM F1295, ASTM F2229, ASTM F1586, ISO 5832-1, ISO 5832-3, ISO 5832-11, ISO 10993, ISO 13485, EN 1441, EN 1811, and REACH compliance. Each entry carries the standard's full name, issuing organization, current year, material type, key chemical requirements, body piercing use, biocompatibility notes, sterilization tolerance, related standards, common uses, important notes, and a verification method.

It also includes a claim verification layer that maps common marketing phrases ("Implant Grade Titanium", "Surgical Steel", "Hypoallergenic", "Medical Grade") to the certifications they require, the certifications that are *not* acceptable substitutes, red flags, and step-by-step verification actions.

### What the tool checks

- Whether a named standard exists in the database and what it actually specifies
- Whether a material is appropriate for a given piercing location and healing stage
- Whether a supplier's phrasing is backed by a specific standard or is unverifiable marketing language
- Which technical questions to send a supplier for a given material and clinical application
- How two certified materials compare side by side
- Galvanic and biocompatibility compatibility between two materials worn together

### What the tool does not do

The tool does not test physical jewelry, does not replace an accredited laboratory test report, and does not certify any specific product. It is a documentation and standards reference. Every result points back to a document you should request from your supplier.

## Who Should Use This Tool

**Studio owners and buyers.** Use the Supplier Question Sheet Generator before placing an order, and the Claim-to-Evidence Matrix when evaluating a new supplier's marketing material. The tool produces printable, sendable question sets rather than vague advice.

**Piercers and apprentices.** Use the Material Safety Checker to understand why a given material is or is not appropriate for a fresh piercing versus a healed one, and for a specific location such as navel, nipple, or oral. Use the Certificate Reader to learn what the phrasing on a jewelry packet actually establishes.

**Front-of-house staff.** When a client asks "is this nickel free?" or "is this implant grade?", the Quick Certification Lookup and Material Safety Checker give a grounded answer instead of a guess.

**Anyone comparing suppliers.** The Certified Material Comparison tool and the Material Mixer module let you put two materials side by side and see elemental composition, galvanic potential difference, and biocompatibility tier.

## How to Use the Biocompatibility Material Checker

The tool is organized as a set of sections reachable from the top navigation: Certificate Reader, Supplier Question Sheet, Claim Matrix, Comparison, Safety Checker, Studio Records, Lookup, and Reference.

### Step 1 - Quick Certification Lookup

Use the **Quick Certification Lookup** section. Type a certification code into the search field, for example `ASTM F136`, `ASTM F138`, `ISO 5832-1`, or `EN 1811`, then press **Search**. The tool matches against the certification database and returns the full technical entry: full standard name, issuing organization, current edition year, material type, key chemical requirements, body piercing use, biocompatibility rating, sterilization tolerance, related standards, common uses, important notes, and the verification method you should ask your supplier for.

The search also matches on keywords, so typing `316LVM`, `nickel release`, `grade 23`, or `biocompatibility` will surface the relevant entries.

### Step 2 - Material Safety Checker

In the **Material Safety Checker** section, fill in the form:

1. **Material Type** (required). Choose from the grouped dropdown: Titanium (Grade 23 / ASTM F136, Grade 5), Steel (316LVM / ASTM F138, 316L non-implant), Precious Metals & Alternatives (Niobium / ASTM F2229, 14K Gold, 18K Gold), Biocompatible Polymers & Glass (BioFlex PP-R, Bioplast, PTFE, Borosilicate Glass), and Other Materials (Natural Stone, Wood / Organics, Acrylic).
2. **Piercing Location.** All locations, Ear, Nose / Septum, Lip / Oral, Eyebrow, Navel, Nipple, Genital, or Surface / Dermal.
3. **Healing Stage.** Initial / Fresh or Healed.
4. **Skin Sensitivity.** Normal or Sensitive / Nickel Allergic.

Press **Check Material Safety**. The result panel renders a safety rating for that combination of material, location, healing stage, and sensitivity.

### Step 3 - Certificate Reader & Claim Analyzer

In the **Certificate Reader & Claim Analyzer** section, either pick one of the **Quick Test Phrasing Presets** or type/paste the supplier's wording into the **Supplier Phrasing / Label Text** box. Example inputs the tool is designed for: `"Implant Grade Titanium Barbell, 100% hypoallergenic, nickel free"`, or a pasted mill certificate snippet.

Press **Analyze Phrasing**. The tool returns what that phrasing establishes, what critical gaps remain, and the documentary proof you should request.

### Step 4 - Supplier Question Sheet Generator

In the **Supplier Question Sheet Generator** section, set:

- **Target Material / Alloy:** ASTM F136 Titanium, ASTM F138 Stainless Steel, BioFlex Medical Polymer (USP Class VI), ASTM F2229 Niobium, Borosilicate Glass 3.3, Solid 14K / 18K Gold, or Commercial 316L / Grade 5 Titanium.
- **Intended Clinical Application:** Initial Piercing, Healed Piercing, Sensitive Skin / Suspected Nickel Allergy, Oral / Mucosal Piercing, or Surface Piercing / Subdermal Dermal Anchor.
- **Your Studio Name**, **Supplier Name**, and **Item / PO Ref** (optional, used to personalize the output).

The tool generates the exact technical questions to send that supplier for that material and application, together with what a satisfactory answer contains and what red flags to avoid. The output is printable and ready to send.

### Step 5 - Claim-to-Evidence Matrix

In the **Claim-to-Evidence Matrix** section, filter claims by category using the pills: **All Claims**, **Metals & Alloys**, **Polymers & Plastics**, **General & Marketing**. Use the search box to filter by claim text or standard. For each claim the matrix shows what document substantiates it, what that document proves, what its absence means, and how to verify it.

### Step 6 - Certified Material Comparison

The **Certified Material Comparison Tool** section puts two certified materials side by side. A separate **Material Mixer** module evaluates two materials worn together: it reads each material's galvanic potential (V vs SCE in 0.9% NaCl), computes the potential difference, and returns a galvanic risk level (low, medium, high), a combined elemental list, and a biocompatibility tier from 1 to 3. A pairing is flagged unsafe when galvanic risk is high or the lower biocompatibility tier is 1, and caution when galvanic risk is medium or the lower tier is 2.

### Step 7 - Recent Checks

The **Recent Checks** panel tracks your session queries and material verifications, shows a running item count, and offers a **Clear History** button. This is session history only, useful for reviewing what you have already looked up during a purchasing session.

## Use Case Examples

### Example 1 - A supplier claims "Implant Grade Titanium"

A supplier's listing reads: *"Implant Grade Titanium Barbell, 100% hypoallergenic, nickel free."*

Paste that text into the **Certificate Reader & Claim Analyzer** and press **Analyze Phrasing**. The tool matches it against the `implant_grade_titanium` claim entry, which requires **ASTM F136** and lists as not acceptable: ASTM B348 (industrial bar), commercial grade titanium, and a certificate naming no specification or heat number. The red flags returned include no mill certification provided, Grade 5 marketed as "implant grade", suspiciously low price, and generic "titanium" without a grade specification. The verification steps returned are: request mill certification showing ASTM F136, verify Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI) rather than Grade 5, check oxygen content is ≤0.13% (not 0.20%), and confirm the ELI designation.

You now have four specific things to ask for, not a general suspicion.

### Example 2 - A client with a suspected nickel allergy

A client wants a navel piercing and mentions reacting to cheap earrings. In the **Material Safety Checker**, set Material Type to **Surgical Steel 316LVM - ASTM F138**, Piercing Location to **Navel**, Healing Stage to **Initial / Fresh**, and Skin Sensitivity to **Sensitive / Nickel Allergic**. Run the check, then run it again with Material Type set to **Titanium Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI) - ASTM F136**.

The ASTM F138 database entry notes it contains 13-15% nickel and is a potential allergen for sensitive individuals, with a `conditional` safety rating. The ASTM F136 entry is rated `safe`, described as hypoallergenic and nickel-free. That contrast is the conversation you have with the client.

### Example 3 - Auditing a new supplier before a first order

You are considering a new supplier for BioFlex jewelry used in initial piercings. In the **Supplier Question Sheet Generator**, set Target Material to **BioFlex Medical Polymer (USP Class VI)**, Intended Clinical Application to **Initial Piercing (Fresh Open Wound Canal)**, and fill in your studio name, the supplier name, and the PO reference. Generate the sheet and send it.

If the supplier cites **EN 1441** for nickel or material safety, the database entry for that standard explains it was a 1997 medical device risk analysis standard, withdrawn and replaced by EN ISO 14971, and sets no nickel limit. The correct document to request instead is an **EN 1811** nickel release test report, which measures against the REACH Annex XVII entry 27 limits of less than 0.2 µg/cm²/week for posts in pierced parts of the body.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. What does the Biocompatibility Material Checker actually do?**
It looks up certification standards (ASTM, ISO, EN, REACH) and material safety data, analyzes supplier phrasing against a claim verification database, generates supplier question sheets, and compares certified materials. It is a documentation and standards reference, not a physical test.

**2. Is "implant grade titanium" a certification?**
No. It is a marketing phrase. The tool's claim database maps it to **ASTM F136** as the required certificate, and lists ASTM B348 industrial bar, commercial grade titanium, and a certificate naming no specification or heat number as not acceptable substitutes.

**3. What is the difference between ASTM F136 and ISO 5832-3?**
ASTM F136 specifies Ti-6Al-4V ELI (Grade 23) with an oxygen limit of 0.13%. ISO 5832-3 specifies Ti-6Al-4V (Grade 5) with an oxygen limit of 0.20%. Both are implant specifications, but they are not the same specification. If you require ASTM F136, an ISO 5832-3 certificate does not show it.

**4. Is 316L steel the same as ASTM F138?**
No. The tool's claim database lists 316L (non-implant grade), 304 stainless, and generic stainless steel as not acceptable for a "Surgical Steel" claim. ASTM F138 specifies 316LVM, vacuum melted, with a carbon maximum of 0.030%.

**5. Does surgical steel contain nickel?**
Yes. The ASTM F138 entry lists nickel at 13.00-15.00% and rates the material `conditional`, noting it is a potential allergen for sensitive individuals. For EU compliance, the relevant document is an EN 1811 nickel release test report.

**6. What is the EN 1811 nickel release limit for piercing jewelry?**
The tool states the REACH Annex XVII entry 27 limits: less than **0.2 µg/cm²/week** for posts inserted into pierced parts of the body, and **0.5 µg/cm²/week** for other articles in direct and prolonged skin contact. Coated articles must stay within the limit for at least two years of normal use, simulated with EN 12472 before the EN 1811 test.

**7. Is a DMG spot test proof of nickel compliance?**
No. The EN 1811 entry notes that a dimethylglyoxime (DMG) spot test is only a screening check under CEN/TR 12471, not proof of compliance. The reference method is EN 1811, with the article sitting one week in artificial sweat at 30 °C and nickel measured by spectrometry.

**8. What does ISO 10993 certify?**
Nothing by itself. The database entry describes ISO 10993 as a testing standard, not a material certification. It defines biocompatibility testing protocols (Part 5 cytotoxicity, Part 10 irritation and skin sensitization, Part 11 systemic toxicity). Look for "ISO 10993 compliant" or "ISO 10993 tested" on documentation.

**9. What does ISO 13485 mean on a supplier's paperwork?**
It means the manufacturer has a quality management system. The database entry states clearly that it does not certify the material itself and is not a substitute for material certifications like ASTM F136.

**10. Can I wear two different materials in the same piercing?**
The Material Mixer module evaluates this. It computes the galvanic potential difference between the two materials and returns a risk level. A pairing is flagged unsafe when the potential difference exceeds 0.35 V or the lower biocompatibility tier is 1, and caution when the difference exceeds 0.15 V or the lower tier is 2. Non-conductive materials such as BioFlex polymer and borosilicate glass have no galvanic potential and do not contribute to galvanic risk.

**11. Does the tool store my data?**
No. The tool runs entirely client-side. The Recent Checks panel is session history only and can be cleared with the **Clear History** button.

**12. Is the tool available in other languages?**
Yes. The interface supports English, French, Italian, German, Spanish, Portuguese, and Dutch via the language selector in the header.
